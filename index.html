Research interests

Quantum dynamics and open systems

An isolated quantum system cannot share information with the external world, and thus evolves unitarily. However, if the system interacts with its environment in a non-negligible way, its evolution is described by the much richer class of CPTP maps. A remarkable case - pervasive in Quantum Optics and thermodynamics - is that of Lindblad dynamics, describing the evolution of a system weakly coupled to a memory-less environment. It is known that under these irreversible dynamics, a system needs an infinite ammount of time to shrink the support of its initial state, i.e. "no purification in finite time". In recent works, we have aimed to make this statement more precise by providing a lower bound to the minimum time to join arbitrary initial and final quatum states under Lindblad dynamics of bounded norm. Our lower bound diverges whenever the initial state is not supported in the final one, significantly improving previous finite lower bounds in the literature. From mathematical point of view, the minimal time between pairs of states becomes a quasi-distance: the time from A to B need not be the same as from B to A, which is to be expected from the inherent irreversibility of Lindblad dynamics. Then, minimum-time conversion tasks can be read as geodesic-finding problem, of interest for Quantum Control Theory.
